Versioning and deprecation

  • The version is in the path: /v1. Within /v1, changes are additive: new endpoints, new optional parameters, new fields in a response and new values in a list of types. Write clients that ignore fields they do not know.
  • A change that could break a working client (removing or renaming a field, changing a type or a default, tightening a limit) goes into a new path version. The old version keeps working for at least 12 months after the new one is released.
  • A deprecated endpoint or parameter is listed here first, and its responses carry a Deprecation header and a Sunset date at least six months ahead.
  • Error code values are stable. Messages may be reworded at any time.
  • Until Release 1.0, a breaking change can still happen. Each one is listed below with a Change tag, with notice to key holders by email.

Mapping versions

Every fact carries mapping_version, the rule set that turned a company's XBRL concept into a metric such as revenue. A patch version fixes a mistake for some companies, a minor version adds tags or metrics, and a major version changes what a metric means. The version of each fact is kept, so a stored value can always be traced to the rules that produced it.

  1. 1.5.0 · 23 Nov 2026

    Revenue, mapping 1.5.0 (23 Nov 2026). Two changes to how revenue is read. Both restate figures we published before; every changed value is listed in the data-quality report, and answers for an earlier date (as_of) carry the mapping version that produced them.

    1. The income-statement line decides. When a filing reports revenue under two tags that disagree, the one the filing shows on its income statement is now the figure, provided it is the only revenue line there and is labelled as a total (“Revenues”, “Total revenues”, “Net sales”). 638 published annual and quarterly values change at 91 companies, and 1,724 values that were marked ambiguous now have a figure. Examples: Republic Services 2022 is $13,511M as on its income statement (we showed $15,645M, a gross figure); Williams 2025 is $11.95B (we showed none). Filings before 2021 are unchanged.
    2. Bank revenue for banks that report in IFRS. For banks and credit institutions, revenue is now the total income line on the income statement, after interest expense, as the bank labels it (“Total operating income”, “Total income”, “Total revenues”), where the filing shows that line. This adds 216 values (55 bank-years) at 10 banks, among them HSBC ($68,274M total operating income, 2025), UBS Group ($49,573M total revenues), Santander (EUR 58,670M total income) and BBVA (2020). This is net of interest expense, so it is not comparable with US banks’ gross revenue figures.

    The pass matched its dry run exactly (0% drift on every count); nothing previously shown lost its figure.

  2. 1.4.0 · 22 Nov 2026

    Total-first revenue; precedence judged within each filing; contract revenue below the total is a component, not a conflict (Berkshire Hathaway's revenue no longer stops at 2017).

  3. 1.3.0 · 22 Nov 2026

    Thirty metrics, industry fallback tags, a unit check, cover-page facts and IFRS.

Changes

  1. 25 Nov 2026
    • ChangeDividends: more companies covered (IFRS filers and partnerships); each value now says its basis (declared, cash paid, recognised in the period, or distributions per unit), and payout ratios are shown only for declared or recognised dividends.
  2. 24 Nov 2026
    • NewExecutive pay versus performance from each company’s proxy: the CEO’s total pay and compensation actually paid, the other named executives’ averages, total shareholder return beside its peer group’s, net income and the measure the company chose, year by year. As disclosed in the proxy, each year linked to it. GET /v1/companies/{cik}/executive-pay.
    • NewInsider trading plans and planned sales: Rule 10b5-1 plans adopted or ended, as companies report them in their 10-Qs and 10-Ks, and Form 144 notices of a planned sale (from the last 12 months), each shown with the Form 4 sale that followed and how we matched it. Information, not signals. GET /v1/companies/{cik}/insider-plans.
    • NewWhat changed in a 10-K: risk factors and MD&A headings added, removed or reworded since the year before, side by side, each linked to its report. Available for the largest US filers so far; for other companies the page says it isn’t available yet. GET /v1/companies/{cik}/changes and /v1/filings/{accession}/changes.
    • NewA calendar of filing deadlines, late filing notices, and IPO lock-up and quiet-period ends, for a company, a watchlist or a list of companies. Dates we work out from rules are marked derived and give the rule.
    • NewSEC comment letters: the SEC’s questions on a company’s filings and its replies, grouped by review, with topics from a reviewed keyword list. Read for the largest filers since 2019; other companies’ letters are marked not read yet.
    • NewCorporate tree: a company’s parents and the entities under it, as reported to GLEIF, and the subsidiaries listed in its annual report.
    • NewPrivate raises: Form D notices of exempt offerings for the last eight quarters, through the latest SEC quarterly data set, searchable by industry, state, amount raised, date and exemption. Self-reported by the issuer on Form D; amounts as filed. GET /v1/private-raises.
    • NewStatements can show shares and earnings per share adjusted for a stock split confirmed from the company’s own filings, marked derived, beside the figures as filed.
    • NewIn the Explorer: a What’s new band on each company’s Overview; Peers that open Compare in one click; a People tab and a Calendar tab; What changed, comment letters and Form D offerings on Filings; the corporate tree on Connections; Private raises in the Screener; an As filed / Split-adjusted switch on Statements. Every view has its own link, ⌘K reaches each one, and “Ask” opens chat about a row.
    • NewWatchlists mark what is new since your last visit and show the list’s calendar.
    • NewChat exports each table as CSV, gives each answer a link that asks again as of the same date, and answers questions on pay, trading plans and Form 144 notices, 10-K changes, deadlines, private raises, SEC comment letters and the corporate tree. MCP tools cover all of these.
    • ChangeCompany search puts the company you most likely mean first: “Google” finds Alphabet, “Coca-Cola” The Coca-Cola Company, “Lucid” Lucid Group.
