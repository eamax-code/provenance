Reading the flags

Marks you can meet next to a figure, what each one means, and an example to open. A mark says how a figure was filed or worked out; it is never a judgement on the company.

Information, not signals. Not investment advice.

Examples marked free key open with a free key; the others open for anyone.

Figures in the statements

derived

Worked out by this service from reported figures (a margin, a ratio, a quarter as the year less its first three quarters); its inputs and their filings are listed with it. Q4 is worked out only for amounts that add up across quarters (revenue, income, cash flows, dividends per share); per-share earnings and share counts never are.

Example: Apple’s operating margin.

As reported

Each figure comes from the latest filing that reports it as of the date shown, which can be a later restated filing; the tooltip names it.

Example: Apple’s income statement.

Shaded cell, before a split

Reported on the share basis before a later stock split, so its per-share figures and share counts do not compare with the columns after it; year-on-year changes skip that pair.

Example: Netflix’s years before its 10-for-1 split of November 2025.

Split-adjusted

Figures before a split put on today’s share basis by this service (derived, in italics), using the reviewed splits listed under the table.

Example: Netflix with Split-adjusted on.

! (a quality note)

The figure did not pass a check as filed; the tooltip says which. It is shown as filed and left out of ratios and screens.

unit doubtful

EPS as filed is about 100 times the same filing’s net income over weighted shares: probably cents or pence tagged as dollars or pounds. The weighted shares it was checked against carry the same note.

Example: Rio Tinto, FY2017 diluted EPS 486.9 free key.

Fiscal year names (tooltip on each column)

FY2025 names a fiscal year as the company’s 10-K does where it says so (Gap’s year ended 31 January 2026 is its fiscal 2025); otherwise by the year it ends in.

Examples: Gap free key; Apple.

Transition period (N months, ended …)

A short period between two fiscal years, reported when the company moved its year end (usually on Form 10-KT). It is never a fiscal year and has no year-on-year change.

Example: Precision Castparts, the 9 months ended 3 January 2016 free key.

Transition Q1, Q2 …

A quarter inside a transition period, numbered from that period’s start, not a quarter of a fiscal year. It is still a quarter, so it compares with the quarter a year before.

Example: the same company, quarterly free key.

Filing status

No periodic report since {form} filed {date}

No 10-K, 10-Q, 20-F or 40-F of the company’s own in about the last 18 months, counted back from the newest filing loaded here. It may have been taken private or merged, or now report under a new registrant; its figures stop at that report. Screens and rankings leave it out by default; its pages stay. At a point in time, the line shows only if this was already true then.

Example: Google Inc. free key.

Files no periodic reports of its own (offerings only)

The registrant files offering documents, such as note prospectuses, but no periodic report of its own; often a subsidiary that issues debt.

Example: JPMorgan Chase Financial Co. LLC free key.

no longer files (Screener)

A Screener row for a company in either case above. The Screener lists them only with Include companies that no longer file ticked.

Example: revenue for 2024 with it ticked free key.

Succeeded by, Merged into, Acquired by

The company the registrant’s own filings name as its successor, with the sentence quoted in the tooltip and the filing linked. Succeeded by: a successor issuer, a new holding company or a change of state, or when unclear. Merged into: it merged with and into the named company. Acquired by: the filing says the named company acquired it.

Shown for reference and never joined: the successor’s figures are not this company’s history, except where a reviewed succession continues them (Successor to, on the successor’s page).

Examples: Google Inc., succeeded by Alphabet Inc. free key; Medco Health Solutions, acquired by Express Scripts Holding Co. free key.

Dividends

As reported

Each amount on the share basis of the filing it cites; turn on Split-adjusted to see them on today’s basis.

Example: Apple.

The basis in the row label

declared: what the board declared for the period. cash paid: paid in the period, which can include the previous year’s final dividend; no payout ratio. recognised in the period (IFRS): what an IFRS filer recognised in the year, typically last year’s final plus this year’s interim. distributions per unit: a partnership’s distributions; no payout ratio.

Examples: Apple, declared; Gladstone Capital, cash paid free key; BHP, recognised free key; CrossAmerica Partners, per unit free key.

Insider trades (Form 4)

Amended (4/A)

A Form 4/A restated this trade; the row shows the amendment’s figures and the tooltip the first-filed ones.

Example: Alphabet, a sale of 30 July 2025.

Cancelled by 4/A

An amendment says the trade did not happen as filed: it shows 0 shares and counts nowhere.

Example: Dropbox, November 2026 free key.

Filed twice

The same trades were filed on two Forms 4; one row, both filings linked.

Example: Netflix, May 2025.

Price looks wrong as filed

The price is far from the company’s other trades that month, often a total typed in the price box; the value is kept as filed and left out of value filters and ranking.

Example: Lucky Strike Entertainment, August 2026 free key.

Value partial

Some lines of the trade had no price, so its value understates it.

Example: BGC Group, 6 October 2025.

Cluster buy

Three or more different buyers bought the company’s shares on the open market within 30 days; insiders who file together or are affiliated count as one buyer. This purchase is one of them.

Example: LCNB Corp, October and November 2026 free key.

Plan purchase

The filing says the shares were bought under a plan (an employee or director stock purchase plan, a plan trust or dividend reinvestment), not on the open market. Such purchases are left out of Buys, cluster buys and first buys.

Example: TSMC, 6 November 2026.

Likely plan purchase (not stated)

Every line is a fraction of a share under 100 shares, as reinvestment, plan and fund-subscription purchases usually are, though the filing does not say so. Treated like a plan purchase.

Example: York Water, 20 November 2026 free key.

Fund managers (Form 13F)

Possible split; not compared

All managers’ shares of the security moved as a stock split would, so this quarter is not compared with the one before.

Example: FMR LLC’s Amphenol position, quarter ended 30 September 2026 free key.

Compared after the 10:1 split

The security split during the quarter, so the shares held before it were put on the new basis before comparing (derived).

Example: Netflix in Funds bought, quarter ended 31 December 2025.

Values look reported in thousands

The filing’s values look 1,000 times too small, as if still reported in thousands after 2023; shown as filed and left out of totals.

Example: Chase Investment Counsel, quarter ended 31 December 2025 free key.

Files notices only

The manager filed a 13F notice; its holdings are reported by the managers it names, listed on its page.

Example: Vanguard Group free key.

Activist stakes (Schedule 13D)

Switched from passive (13G) to active (13D)

The filer, or someone filing with it, first reported the stake on a Schedule 13G, the form for passive holders, and then filed a 13D, the form required when the holder may seek to change or influence control of the company, or no longer qualifies for the short form. The 13G is linked.

Example: Repay Holdings, Forager Fund free key.

{lead filer} and N others (group)

Several reporting persons filed together as a group; the first named is the lead filer. The percent is the one the filing gives, never added up across members: members often each report the group’s shares they are deemed to own, so adding them would count shares twice.

Example: Saba Capital Management and 2 others on BlackRock TCP Capital, 8.46% free key.

Item 4, stated in …

The stated purpose is quoted as filed from Item 4 of an earlier filing in the chain, because the latest amendment left Item 4 unchanged or said it does not apply. It is never summarised; the filing it comes from is linked.

Example: Sunrise Realty Trust, Leonard M. Tannenbaum free key.

The percent shown is one member’s

When no single cover page reached 5%, the percent shown is the largest single reporting person’s. These filings give no group total, so the group’s own stake can’t be read from them.

Example: FONAR, Timothy Raymond Damadian and 56 others, under Include exited stakes free key.

Exited

The latest filing reports under 5% after the stake was 5% or more, or no shares at all; the filings before it stay listed with it.

Example: Liberty Media on Live Nation Entertainment, under Include exited stakes free key.

What is covered

13D filings in the SEC’s structured format, December 2024 onward. Earlier and free-text filings are not read: for example, the Schedule 13D on Match Group filed 15 July 2024 is not shown.

Example: Activist stakes across companies.

Information, not signals. A 13D says what the filer reported, not what it will do.

Fails to deliver (SEC)

A running balance

The SEC’s published balance of shares that had failed to settle and were still outstanding on each settlement date, across all clearing members. It is not the day’s new fails, it doesn’t show how old the fails are, and it is not a measure of short selling.

Example: Apple.

Price —

The SEC file gives no price for that day, or one under a penny. The dash is not a zero.

Example: 1606 Corp. free key.

Filing text

Quoted as filed; not summarised

The reader shows a 10-K item’s text exactly as the filing gives it, in its paragraphs, with its own subheadings as headings. Nothing is reworded or shortened, and the document on sec.gov is linked above it.

Example: Apple’s risk factors.
